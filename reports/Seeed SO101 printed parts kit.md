# Order fast, then verify before you build

**Yes, buying the Seeed SO-ARM101 printed parts from RobotShop US ($54.78 delivered) is a reasonable choice, but only if you confirm it actually ships within about a week.** RobotShop is a long-running retailer with no sign of financial trouble. The listing is sold by RobotShop itself, not a marketplace vendor, and it shows "In stock" in the US. So if the parts really are sitting in a North American warehouse, they should reach San Diego well before Nov 15. The main risk is that "In stock" sometimes turns out to mean "we'll order it from Seeed": the UK version of the same listing is on backorder, and several 2026 buyers say "in stock" items shipped from China. The parts should work with your setup. They are standard SO-101 parts that run with stock LeRobot (`so101_follower`), and your third-party STS3215 servos and non-Seeed board don't matter to the software. Two things need checking: the board mount is probably shaped for Seeed's own driver board, so yours may not fit, and nobody has published whether Seeed's "fine-tuned" geometry exactly matches the official URDF that your IK will rely on. You will also pay for 12 leader parts and 12 clamp parts you don't need. Seeed sells the same kit directly for about $30, which is worth comparing at checkout.

## The follower set looks complete, but expect a tight fit and no screws

RobotShop lists **11 white follower parts, 12 black leader parts and 12 grey clamp parts**, all PLA+. Follower parts are printed at 30% infill, twice the community default of 15%, which makes them stiffer and gives screws more to grip ([RobotShop](https://www.robotshop.com/products/seeedstudio-so-arm101-ai-arm-3d-printed-parts)). The official SO-101 design has 9 parts shared by both arms plus 2 follower-only parts (moving jaw and wrist-roll follower), which adds up to exactly 11 ([TheRobotStudio README](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100)). That strongly suggests the white parts make up a full follower arm, including a board mounting plate. This is an inference from the count; I found no Seeed packing list.

No vendor lists screws or the driver board as being in the box. Unmanned Tech says "Servos and electronics are not included" ([Unmanned Tech](https://www.unmannedtechshop.co.uk/products/so-arm101-low-cost-ai-arm-3d-printed-skeleton)), and the Seeed board ships with Seeed's motor kit instead ([Pakronics](https://www.pakronics.com.au/products/new-so-arm101-low-cost-ai-arm-servo-motor-kit-for-lerobot-without-3d-printed-parts-ss114993666)). Assume you need your own fasteners: **M2x6 for motor mounting and M3x6 for horns and joining arm segments** ([OpenELab assembly guide](https://openelab.io/blogs/seeed-studio/build-your-own-so-101-robot)).

I found no independent review that focuses on Seeed's print quality. Seeed's own page shows 92% from 11 reviews, but the review text didn't load ([Seeed](https://www.seeedstudio.com/SO-101-3D-printed-Enclosure-p-6428.html)). The closest real report comes from a Japanese builder who bought from Akizuki Denshi, one of Seeed's sales channels. They said servo pockets are "molded to exactly the right size", so pushing motors in takes force. They also found burrs that blocked screw holes and recommended scraping contact surfaces and test-fitting screws before final assembly ([LAplust](https://note.com/laplust/n/n1ce78dcea58b?hl=en)). A PartaBot buyer (printer unknown) had screws stripping the plastic and needed 10 mm M3 screws instead ([PartaBot](https://partabot.com/products/so-arm101)). Your RCmall servos are probably clones. Their cases should match the standard STS3215 size, but nobody has reported how clones fit in Seeed's pockets.

## "Fine-tuned" means a different board mount, and possibly nothing else

Seeed says only that the parts are "fine-tuned for easier installation", "feature a Servo Adaptor Board for Seeed Studio XIAO", and "will be slightly different than the official documents on Github" ([RobotShop](https://www.robotshop.com/products/seeedstudio-so-arm101-ai-arm-3d-printed-parts)). Seeed has published no changelog, modified STL files or diff. Seeed's own SO-101 wiki links straight to TheRobotStudio's official STLs and describes the standard assembly order ([Seeed wiki](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/lerobot_so100m_new/)). That suggests the changes are tolerances plus a board mount, not new arm geometry. This is my inference; Seeed doesn't say so.

**Board mount.** The official design includes a `WaveShare_Mounting_Plate_SO101`, and a separate Seeed plate was added to the repo in September 2025 after a user pointed out that kits had switched to Seeed's XIAO board ([GitHub issue #131](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100/issues/131)). Seeed's board measures 42 x 25 mm ([The Pi Hut](https://thepihut.com/products/bus-servo-driver-board-for-seeed-xiao)). If your board is a Waveshare or another design, it may not fit the Seeed mount. The board doesn't hold any structure, so you can print the official Waveshare plate or tape or zip-tie the board to the base.

**LeRobot.** Seeed's wiki uses the stock commands `lerobot-setup-motors --robot.type=so101_follower` and `lerobot-calibrate`, with the same motor IDs as Hugging Face's docs ([Seeed wiki](https://wiki.seeedstudio.com/lerobot_so100m_new/); [HF docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/so101)). LeRobot only needs a serial port that speaks the Feetech protocol, so **any compatible driver board works the same in software**. If you use a Waveshare board, set both jumpers to the B channel (USB) ([HF docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/so101)).

**URDF and IK.** This matters most for your project. The official `so101_new_calib.urdf` was generated from the official CAD ([Simulation/SO101](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100/tree/main/Simulation/SO101)). LeRobot calibration absorbs small offsets in where joints sit, but URDF-based IK depends on the real link lengths and joint axis positions. I found **no report of Seeed parts changing link lengths, and no measurement confirming they don't**. The same README also notes that LeRobot's 0–100 gripper scale is not yet reflected in the URDF ([Simulation/SO101](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100/tree/main/Simulation/SO101)). You'll handle that mapping yourself whichever parts you buy.

Seeed sells the same printed-parts kit for **$29.90 as an add-on** ([Seeed motor kit page](https://www.seeedstudio.com/SO-ARM101-Low-Cost-AI-Arm-Kit-p-6426.html)) or **$30.99 on its own** ([Seeed](https://www.seeedstudio.com/SO-101-3D-printed-Enclosure-p-6428.html)), and offers a US-warehouse option at checkout. I couldn't see its US stock, shipping fee or transit time. Because it's the same Seeed product, you'd face the same board-mount and URDF questions. The real comparison is total cost and delivery date at checkout: if Seeed's US warehouse has it and shipping is under about $25, Seeed wins on price. Waveshare sells resin (not PLA+) printed parts ([Waveshare](https://www.waveshare.com/so-arm100-3dp-parts-kit.htm)). Other sellers such as PartaBot and ThinkRobotics mostly sell full arm kits. Your other option is to have a local print shop print only the official follower STLs. You'd then get exactly the URDF geometry and the Waveshare plate, and you wouldn't pay for leader and clamp parts you don't need. The notes didn't cover local print-shop prices or turnaround, so compare that yourself.

## Conclusion

The risk here is not RobotShop or Seeed's part quality. It's a mismatch between what you need and what the kit is designed for. The kit is aimed at buyers with Seeed's motor kit and Seeed's board who will use LeRobot teleoperation, where small geometry differences are absorbed by calibration. You're building a single follower arm with your own board and URDF-based IK, which is the one use where an undocumented "fine-tuning" could quietly throw off your measurements. That makes measuring the joint axes after assembly part of the build, not an optional extra, whichever vendor you choose.

Because of your deadline, order today, ask RobotShop about stock right away, and set a firm cancel-and-switch date of Oct 30. That leaves about two weeks for a Seeed-direct or print-shop backup. Spending $55 to get within a week of a working arm is a good trade, as long as you don't let a vague "In stock" label use up the slack in your schedule.
